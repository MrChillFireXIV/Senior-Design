**Economic**
The project has an estimated total cost of approximately $600–$1,500, with the shear strain gauges being one of the largest expenses at approximately $150–$350.  
To control costs, the design will use a free database as well as having bumper plates that can also be borrowed from the gym for testing.

**Security**
The system will associate workout sets with individual user accounts and stores information such as weight, rep count, peak velocity, and range of motion.
The design limits the bar's exposure to account information by keeping accounts and passwords on the phone rather than storing them on the cuff, while the phone sends workout data to the cloud over HTTPS.
The NFC system also uses a feature that checks for expiration, reducing the risk that an old tap link could be reused to start another user's session.

**Ethical**
The project has an ethical responsibility to avoid presenting inaccurate weight or workout measurements as reliable results, so the design targets approximately ±2.5 lbs total accuracy and requires calibration and post-drop checks.
The system measures loaded barbells from lifters during squats, bench presses and deadlifts, so incorrect measurements could affect how users understand their training.
The design balances this requirement against cost by using relatively inexpensive prototype materials while reserving the more durable aluminum enclosure and full sensor implementation for the final version.

**Professional**
The project requires specialized expertise because the strain gauges must be bonded to the bar correctly and the cuff must be calibrated through multiple loading conditions.
The team must rely on appropriate engineering and laboratory knowledge rather than treating the prototype as only a software project.
